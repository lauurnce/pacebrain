# Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures — Breiman, 2001

Contrasts a data-modelling culture assuming a stochastic model and an
algorithmic culture optimising predictive accuracy.

Argues the first constrains itself to models simple enough to reason about and
pays for it in accuracy, while treating its assumptions as established.

Twenty-five years on the algorithmic side clearly won on prediction, and the
essay's warning about opacity has become the interpretability literature. Worth
reading for the framing of the disagreement rather than the verdict.
